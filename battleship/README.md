# battleship/

Sea Battle (Battleship) as a data-driven library: rulesets (boards with islands, straight and polyomino ships, fleets,
touching, shots per turn, what a sinking announces, the halo, special weapons) as plain JSON, a pure engine with hidden
fleets, seeded layouts and placement strategies, a solver that reasons about every fleet consistent with what is known,
bots with methods, personalities and levels, simulations with ratings, a game notation with the information of every
shot, style analysis and achievements, and the game definition for [`rooms/`](../rooms/). Pure ES modules, no DOM: the
same code runs in browsers, on the rooms server, in simulations and inside bot searches. The application is
[`battleship-game/`](../battleship-game/).

## Rulesets

A ruleset is JSON: `{ id, name, description, author, tags, exclusive, board, shapes, fleet, rules, weapons, skin? }`.
`name` / `description` may be a string or `{ en, ru, … }`. `normalizeRuleset` fills every default, resolves the shapes,
expands the fleet into `types` (one per fleet entry) and `ships` (one per ship, the largest first) and computes `hash` —
the first 8 hex digits of SHA-256 of the canonical game-relevant content (names, descriptions and looks excluded): two
rulesets with the same hash play the same game.

```js
board:  { width: 10, height: 10, obstacles: [[x, y], …] },          // 4…24 × 4…24; obstacles (islands) hold no ship
shapes: [{ id: 'L', name, cells: [[0, 0], [0, 1], [0, 2], [1, 2]], rotate: true, mirror: true }],   // own polyominoes
fleet:  [{ shape: 'I4', count: 1, name: 'battleship' }, …],          // built-in I1…I8, L3, O4, T4, S4, L4, P5, U5, X5, T5, L5, V5, D6
```

A shape is any 4-connected polyomino of up to 12 cells inside 8 × 8; `orientations(shape)` gives its distinct
rotations (when `rotate`) and mirror images (when `mirror`). Cells are numbered row by row (`cell = y · width + x`) and
named like the squared paper of the school game: a column letter and a row number (`a1` is the top left corner).

| rule | default | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `minPlayers`, `maxPlayers` | 2, 2 | up to 6: free-for-all |
| `teams` | 0 | number of teams (seat *i* plays for team *i* mod *teams*): partners never fire at each other |
| `touch` | `'none'` | `'none'` (not even at the corners), `'corners'` (corners may touch, sides may not), `'any'` |
| `shots` | `'single'` | `'salvo'` (one shot per ship afloat), `'volley'` (a fixed number: `volley`, 3) |
| `afterHit` | `'again'` | a hit earns another shot, or `'pass'` |
| `announce` | `'ship'` | `'ship'` (a sunk ship is shown), `'sunk'` ("sunk" and the type, cells not shown), `'hit'` (only hit or miss) |
| `halo` | true | the water around a shown sunk ship is marked (needs `announce: 'ship'` and touching forbidden) |
| `first` | `'random'` | or `'seat0'` |

Weapons are rule modules, off by default (charges per player): `radar` (how many ship cells a `radarSize` × `radarSize`
square holds), `airstrike` (a row or a column of `airstrikeLength` cells), `cluster` (a cross of five cells), `mines`
(hidden on the own board; a shot into a mine strikes back at the same cell of the shooter's board and ends the turn).
`validateRuleset` (placement.js) also proves that the fleet fits at all with the touching rule.

## Engine

`newGame(ruleset, { players, rng, first })` starts in the `place` phase; states are plain JSON. `apply(state, seat,
action)` validates and returns `{ state, events, error }` without changing its input. Actions: `{ type: 'place', ships:
[{ t, o, x, y }], mines }`, `{ type: 'fire', target, cells }` (one cell, or the whole salvo / volley: `dueFor`),
`{ type: 'radar' | 'cluster', target, cell }`, `{ type: 'airstrike', target, cell, dir }`, `{ type: 'resign' }`. Every
cell of an action is answered (a salvo that sinks the last ship still answers its other cells). The battle begins when
every fleet is placed; the last fleet (or team) afloat wins.

Public marks of a board: 0 unknown, 1 miss, 2 hit, 3 hit of a shown sunk ship, 4 water marked by the halo, 5 an exploded
mine. The public log (`state.log`, in every view) records `['P', seat]` placed, `['T', seat]` a turn, `['F', seat,
target, n]` a fire action, `['S', shooter, target, cell, ans, t?, cells?]` a shot (0 miss, 1 hit, 2 sunk — with the type,
and the cells when ships are shown — 3 mine), `['K', owner, victim, cell, ans, …]` a mine striking back, `['H', board,
cells]` the halo, `['R' | 'A' | 'C', seat, target, cell, …]` weapons, `['O', seat]` a fleet lost, `['Q', seat]`
resigned, `['E']` the end. `viewOf(state, seat)` shows the own fleet and mines, every board's public marks and sunk ships
(with what their announcement revealed), the charges, the radar counts and the log; spectators (seat −1) see no fleet; at
the end every fleet is open. `undo(state, seat)` replays the game without `seat`'s last shot (take-backs of local
practice games).

## Presets

Classic: **Classic** (the school game: 4, 3 × 2, 2 × 3, 1 × 4, no touching, a hit earns another shot, sunk ships shown
with their halo), **Battleship** (Hasbro: 5, 4, 3, 3, 2, ships may touch, turns alternate, the type of a sunk ship is
named), **Salvo**, **Three-shot volley**, **Quick 8 × 8**, **Mini 6 × 6**, **Fog of war** (only hit or miss), **Armada
14 × 14**, **Ocean 20 × 20** (salvos), **Free-for-all** (3–4 players), **Two against two** (teams).

Exclusives designed for this engine:

- **Archipelago** — 12 × 10 with thirteen islands that bend the probabilities around them.
- **Tetromino fleet** — polyomino ships (I, L, T, S, O and two corner tugs) that turn and mirror: a hit no longer tells
  which way the hull goes.
- **Arsenal** — the classic game with two radar scans, an air strike, a cluster bomb and two mines per player.
- **Deep Sonar** — 12 × 12 in the fog (no sinkings announced) with three sonar scans: information is the weapon.

## Placement

`randomLayout(rs, rng, { strategy, strength })`: `uniform` (every legal layout equally likely: a Markov chain from a
random start), `random` (one ship after another, what a quick human "random" does), `edges`, `sea` (away from the edges),
`spread`, `cluster`, `antiDensity` (where a density hunter looks last: the placement counts of an empty board,
`huntDensity`); the clever strategies pick the best of 1 + 24 × `strength` uniform candidates, so they stay random.
Mines go where shooters are drawn. `occupancy(rs)` is the probability that a cell holds a ship under uniform layouts — with
no touching allowed the corners and edges are *likelier* than the centre, while density hunters start in the centre.

## Solver

The knowledge of a board (`knowledge.js`) is the solver's input: cells known to be water (misses, the halo, exploded mines,
islands), known hits, the sunk ships with what their announcement revealed (the cells; or the type, the cell and the hits
at that moment), radar counts. A *configuration* places every ship of the fleet (sunk ones too) so that no two ships break
the touching rule, no ship covers known water, every hit is covered, every radar count matches, each sunk ship lies where
its announcement allows and no ship still afloat is already fully hit.

- `sample` — Monte-Carlo fleets: a Gibbs sampler over ship placements (each move puts one ship back uniformly among the
  places consistent with the others), joint moves of two ships (one long ship ↔ two short ones over the same hits; ships
  jammed in a tight space), several chains from randomized backtracking; small positions are enumerated exactly instead.
  Checked against exact enumeration in the tests (largest error over every cell ≈ 0.04 with 2000 samples).
- `enumerate` — every configuration, within a node budget: a search tree that covers uncovered hits first and places
  interchangeable ships in increasing order, so every configuration is exactly one leaf.
- `estimateLog2` — log₂ of the number of consistent fleets: exact when the enumeration fits, otherwise Knuth's estimate
  of the number of leaves of that tree (random descents; the mean of the products of the branching factors — unbiased, and
  hits are covered by construction).
- `density` — the classic heuristic: placements of the ships still afloat through each cell.
- `endgame` — exact expectimax over a small list of configurations: the minimal expected number of shots (or of misses,
  when a hit earns another shot) to sink everything.

## Bots

A bot is a profile: a shooting **method**, a **placement** strategy, a **targeting** rule for several opponents, traits
and a level.

| method | |
| --- | --- |
| `random` | random shots, a naive follow-up of hits (by `discipline`) |
| `hunt` | hunt and target: random search, then the neighbours of a hit and along the line |
| `parity` | hunt on the checkerboard of the smallest ship of two cells or more still afloat |
| `density` | the placement-density heuristic |
| `montecarlo` | sampled consistent fleets: the cell most likely to hold a ship |
| `information` | the same samples: the cell whose answer carries the most expected information |
| `solver` | exact enumeration as soon as the position is small, exact expectimax of the endgame, Monte Carlo otherwise |

| trait | effect |
| --- | --- |
| `discipline` | finishes a wounded ship before searching on |
| `curiosity` | weight of the expected information against the chance to hit |
| `edgeBias` | prior of the search: the open sea first (0), none (0.5), the edges first (1) |
| `trigger` | weapons fired at once (1) or kept for the end (0) |
| `cunning` | how hard it optimises its placement strategy |
| `chaos` | noise on its scores |

Targeting with several opponents: `best` (the best chance anywhere), `weakest`, `leader`, `random`, `revenge` (whoever
fired at it last). Weapons are weighed by their expected gain (expected hits of an air strike or a cluster bomb from the
samples; the entropy of a radar count); mines are avoided by their estimated chance. Levels: Easy (30 % random blunders,
often forgets a wounded ship, no sampling), Normal (10 %, 120 samples), Hard (3 %, 300 samples, exact small positions),
Expert (no blunders, 800 samples, exact endgames). Big seas scale the samples down by the work of a sweep; a decision
stays well below a second on 20 × 20. The personality (method, placement, traits) is the same at every level.

Personalities (`TEMPLATES`, each with a summary and three principles): **Rookie** (random shots, quick placement),
**Hunter** (checkerboard hunt and target, presses the weakest), **Cartographer** (placement density, spread-out fleet),
**Statistician** (Monte-Carlo fleets, uniform placement no prior can exploit), **Scholar** (maximal information, radar
first, ships in the open sea), **Corsair** (ships along the edges, searches the edges first, attacks the leader),
**Ghost** (anti-density placement, patient weapons), **Admiral** (everything: exact endgames, weapons by expected gain,
anti-density placement). `profileOf('custom', { level, method, placement, targeting, traits })` builds any mix.

### Ratings and interactions (method)

`node battleship/sim.js all --games 300` plays the Classic preset with worker threads (`botstats.js` records the date,
the reference and the numbers):

- **Calibration**: each personality at each level against the reference (Hunter, Hard) in two-player games, 300 games per
  cell; seats alternate and every cell uses the same seeds (common random numbers: the same fleets and the same first
  shooter), so differences come from the bots. The win rate *p* becomes a rating `1500 + 400 · log10(p / (1 − p))`
  (1500 = as good as the reference; +400 = ten times the odds).
- **Duels**: every pair of personalities head to head at Normal — who beats whom.
- **Searches**: how many shots each method (Hard) needs to sink a whole fleet placed by each strategy — which placements
  fool which shooters.
- **Styles**: the decisions of each personality (Hard) through `analysis.js`: the profiles a person's style is compared
  with.

## Notation (SBN) and information

One line per game, `;`-separated fields:

```
SBN1;r=classic@b8703da5;n=2;p=Ann,Admiral~admiral.3;f=0;m=0:e5. 1:c3x 1:c4x 1:c5#1 1:a7. 0:d4x …;F=0.0.0.0_1.0.6.0_…/…;s=seed:amber-dune-41;x=W1
```

`r` ruleset id and hash · `R` the ruleset itself (deflate + base64url) for own rulesets · `n` players · `p` names (a bot is
`name~kind.level`) · `f` who fired first · `m` moves: `<seat>[><target]:<cell><answer>,…` (a shot, a salvo, a volley),
`R<cell>=<count>` radar, `A<cell><h|v>:…` air strike, `C<cell>:…` cluster, `Q` resigned; answers `.` miss, `x` hit,
`#<type>` sunk (`(cells)` when touching ships are shown), `*<answer>` a mine and what it struck back at · `F` the fleets
(`t.o.x.y` per ship, mines after `~`) · `s` seed · `x` winners · `g` date. With the fleets `replay()` runs the real engine and
checks every answer (a tampered record is caught); without them it rebuilds what everybody saw.

**Information**: the answer to a shot carries −log₂ *P*(answer) bits, where *P* is the share of the consistent fleets
(before the shot) that give that answer. By the chain rule the bits of a board add up to log₂ of the number of fleets it
could hide at the start — the entropy of the remaining consistent placements falls by exactly the information of each
answer. The tests check it: on fully sunk boards the summed bits agree with Knuth's estimate of the starting count
(Classic ≈ 50.7 bits, Battleship ≈ 34.8, Quick 8 × 8 ≈ 26.9, Mini 6 × 6 ≈ 17.3). `analysisSteps` gives per shot the
chance of a hit at the chosen cell, the best chance available, the bits of the answer and its expected information; per
board the entropy curve; per seat **luck** (hits above what the chances promised, Σ hit − *P*) and **skill** (mean of
*P*(chosen) / max *P*).

## Player analysis

`observe(counters, view, seat, action)` records one decision, `observePlacement` one fleet; `features(counters)`:
discipline (shots next to a wounded ship), parity (searching on one colour of the checkerboard), edge (searching shots on
the edge), deduction (shots that could still hit), choice (the chance of the chosen cell against the best), curiosity (its
expected information against the best), trigger (weapons fired early), and the placement: ship cells on the edge, spread,
horizontal ships. `compare(features, profiles)` ranks the personalities, `traitsOf` gives badges, `gameSummary` +
`ACHIEVEMENTS` (24, none for practice games with take-backs) the achievements.

## Rooms

`game` (`game.js`) is the [`rooms/`](../rooms/) definition: `settings = { rulesetId | ruleset, skin, names }` (the
ruleset and the room's look travel to every client), the fleets stay in the state — on the server for online rooms — and
every seat and spectator gets only its view (`viewOf`), referring to the ruleset by hash (`hydrate(view)` puts it back).
Bots (`BOT_KINDS`: every personality and `custom`) run where the room runs and see only their views.

## Tests

`node battleship/test.js` (about 45 s; `--long` plays more): every preset validates; shapes and orientations; placements,
touching rules and mines; every strategy on every preset; the engine move by move (a shot again after a hit, sinking and
the halo, secret sinkings, salvos and volleys, radar, air strike, cluster bomb, mines, teams, resigning, hidden fleets in
views, undo); the sampler against exact enumeration and Knuth's estimate against exact counts; the endgame expectimax;
bot games on every preset with every personality (no illegal decision, every game finished), determinism per seed; a small
simulation against the reference; SBN round trips on every preset (full and public replays agree, a tampered answer is
caught, own rulesets travel inside the record) and the chain rule of the bits; style features and achievements; a local
room and a networked game through `rooms/server.js` (password, invite, the room's look, an own ruleset, hidden fleets).
